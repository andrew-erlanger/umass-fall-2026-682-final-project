Answering some questions takes two passages. Rerankers score them one at a time.

We compare four ways to rank the candidate passages for a multi-hop question, and test when a graph over the passages helps find all the evidence.

One question, its candidates, one ranking

Each question arrives with a fixed set of candidate passages. A few are gold: together they contain the answer. The rest are distractors.

This is the cosine baseline's ranking. Move the cutoff to change how many passages the language model gets to read.

recall@k gives partial credit. all_gold@k is 1 only if every gold passage makes the cut. That is our headline metric, because a reader missing one hop cannot answer.

Why a gold passage can rank low

MuSiQue records how each question decomposes into single-hop steps. Each step is answered by one gold passage.

A later step often depends on an entity that the question never names. Its passage shares few words with the question, so a scorer that looks at one passage at a time has little to go on.

That passage is often similar to the other gold passage. A graph over the passages can carry that signal.

Where reranking sits

Only the reranker changes between our four systems. The candidates, the cutoff and the language model stay fixed, so differences in the answer come from the ranking.

We measure twice: the ranking itself, then the answers a frozen language model produces from the top passages.

Three datasets

All three give every question its own small candidate set with labelled gold passages, so no retrieval index is needed.

The official test sets are unlabelled. We hold out 10% of train as validation and report on dev.

Four rerankers, differing in what each one is allowed to see

All four take the same question and candidates and return one score per passage. They differ in whether the question meets the passage's words, and whether passages can inform each other.

What each scorer sees

Cosine compresses the question and each passage separately, then compares the two vectors.

The cross-encoder reads the question and one passage together. It is far sharper, and still blind to the other passages.

The GNN starts from the cosine reranker's vectors and lets each passage hear from its neighbours before scoring.

The hybrid is the same GNN with the cross-encoder's score added to every node.

From twenty loose passages to a graph a network can read

The GNN needs to know which passages are related. We build that graph from the passages alone, then let a small attention network pass information along its edges.

Build the graph

Every passage links to its k most similar passages by embedding cosine. A link counts in both directions. Our setting is k = 3.

Gold labels are never used to choose edges, and the question is not a node. Nearby dots are similar passages.

Drag k and watch the trained GNN re-rank. At k = 0 no passage can hear another.

What a node holds

Each node starts as 769 numbers: the passage's embedding, plus one number for how similar the passage is to the question.

That last number is the only place the question enters the GNN. The hybrid appends a second one, the cross-encoder's raw score.

Who listens to whom

In a graph attention layer, each passage rebuilds its own representation as a weighted mix of itself and its neighbours. The weights are learned.

Select a passage. Edge thickness shows how much attention it pays to each neighbour in the trained model's first layer, averaged over the four heads.

The network and how it learns

Two attention layers. The first mixes neighbours into a 256-number state per passage. The second mixes again and outputs one score.

Two layers means a passage can be influenced by passages up to two edges away.

Training asks one question of every passage: is it gold? The best epoch is the one with the highest all_gold@5 on validation. Dev is never used for selection.

Three experiments, one per research question

Each figure below is the kind of result we will report. The numbers in them come from a small local run and only show the shape of the analysis.

RQ1

Does the hybrid beat its parts?

Train all four rerankers on the same split, score the same dev questions, compare on the same metrics.

Two extra rows calibrate the table. Random is the floor. The GNN with no edges shows how much of the GNN's score comes from message passing at all.

RQ2

Does it matter whether gold passages are linked?

Sort every dev question by how its gold passages sit in the graph, then compare the rerankers inside each group.

One workable definition, used here: look only at edges that join two gold passages. All gold in one piece is connected, none joined is disconnected, anything between is partial.

If graph rerankers work the way we think, their advantage should be largest on the left and vanish on the right.

RQ3

How fragile is the graph?

Corrupt the worked example's graph and watch the two graph rerankers respond. The cross-encoder never sees the graph, so its ranking cannot move.

Delete removes a share of the edges at random. Insert adds that many random edges between unlinked passages.

The models are not retrained. This tests what happens when the graph at test time is worse than the graph the model learned on.

The same test over every dev question

Repeat the corruption on all dev questions, at each noise level, and track all_gold@5.

The cross-encoder and cosine lines are flat by construction. The question is how far the graph rerankers fall, and whether the hybrid's extra signal cushions it.

Still to decide

Open choices that change what the final figures mean.

ChoiceOptions
Connectivity definition (RQ2)Gold-to-gold edges only, as here, or also count gold passages joined through a distractor.
Noise at train time (RQ3)Corrupt only the test graph, as here, or also retrain on corrupted graphs.
Hybrid's training scoresThe cross-encoder scores the same questions it was trained on, so its scores look better in training than on dev. Scoring train out-of-fold would fix this.
Attention layer typeWith the current layer, two passages that share the same neighbourhood get exactly the same score, so the GNN cannot rank inside a tight cluster. A skip connection or the GATv2 layer would let each passage keep its own signal.
